# ATM10 8.5 turbine balance

The pinned pack uses ten thermal Joules per mB of steam and 2.5 Joules per FE.
A full 28-blade turbine therefore produces four FE per consumed mB. Electrical
conversion belongs at the turbine output; water and reactor heat calculations
continue to use thermal Joules.

| Setting | ATM10 8.5 | Fork |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blades supported per coil | 8 | 8 |
| Vent flow | 43478.262 mB/t | 43478.262 mB/t |
| Disperser flow | 1280 mB/t | 1280 mB/t |
| Condensation per condenser | 128000 mB/t | 128000 mB/t |
| Energy capacity per volume | 16000000 J | 6400000 FE |
| Steam capacity per cavity volume | 6400 mB | 6400 mB |
| Full-rotor steam energy | 10 J/mB | 4 FE/mB |
| Additional flow divisor | None | 1 |

The port retains its existing `joulesPerSteam` and `steamDivisor` configuration
keys, with corrected defaults and matching pack migration. The divisor participates
in both generation and maximum-production reporting. The output multiplier
converts Joules to FE once, then scales by effective blades. The live production
report contains the FE actually inserted in the last tick.

Generation uses the integer mB actually extracted, rather than calculating energy
from a fractional flow and extracting only its integer part. Sub-FE output carries
to the following tick. The remainder shares the fuel/energy transaction, persists
in the turbine multiblock cache, and defaults to zero for older saves. Cache merging
retains the maximum recorded remainder rather than adding duplicate snapshots.
The ordinary pressure curve and flow limits remain in effect. A full energy buffer
consumes no steam. The transfer budget also respects the integer NeoForge operation
limit while the storage capacity remains a long integer.

The condenser recovers only the water that fits. A full water tank must not stop
steam conversion: the pinned reference discards excess condensate rather than
rolling back steam and energy. This also corrects the port's all-or-nothing water
insertion, which stopped disconnected turbines once their condensers filled.

## Runtime verification

The isolated ATM11 fixture builds two physical 17×18×17 turbines. One has all
28 blades; the other has one blade on its rotor and therefore generates 1/7 FE
per consumed mB. Both start with a full steam cavity and a full condenser tank.
Twenty ordinary ticks must conserve consumed steam, stored FE and fractional
credit. The fixture also checks full-buffer stalling, zero idle production,
transaction rollback and the restored capacity above the integer FE range.

A controlled checkpoint drains steam and stores 12345 FE while retaining the
remainder generated by the real ticks. A fresh JVM must restore the exact energy
and remainder, then resume generation with that credit. The full pack passed **319 seed checks** and **101 fresh-JVM restart checks**,
with identical mod hashes. The one-blade checkpoint retained 12345 FE plus
0.571428571263823 FE of earned credit. After restart, a normal generation tick
produced a total stored 2155202 FE and retained 0.7142857140861452 FE, satisfying
the steam/energy conservation assertion. The full rotor resumed at 60012345 FE.
Both processes exited normally. See `turbine-balance-runtime/` for results,
checks, input hashes, process identities and raw-log hashes. All three tested
Core, Generators and QA JARs matched their local build artifacts. The main build
passed 79 Java tests and the migration suite passed six Python tests.

External valve transfer, connected coolant recirculation, heat-generator balance,
active/hot reactor persistence and the remaining equipment/progression/addon work
are separate requirements still needed for full parity.

## Reference

The pinned client archive and SHA are recorded in [the baseline](README.md).
The relevant source is the official
[10.7.19.85 turbine implementation](https://github.com/mekanism/Mekanism/blob/v1.21.1-10.7.19.85/src/generators/java/mekanism/generators/common/content/turbine/TurbineMultiblockData.java).
The legacy code itself used fractional rate before whole-mB extraction; the fork
preserves its intended energy ratio while keeping the new transaction accounting
conservative.
